Key

A word or a glyph carries each state. Colour is a second cue.

      What this trainer builds — the instinct to think several moves ahead and learn the switching routine: read the yard, line the road, call clean moves, win in the fewest. The next level — out on the ground — is minimizing the actual distance you run and using the lead as working room. That's real-yard efficiency that comes with seat time, beyond what this tool scores.

      How to play & why these rules

      You are the conductor, not the engineer. Click a switch target to line it (yellow = lined into the track / diverging, green = normal, straight up the ladder). Then call a move:

      • PULL n from a track — back in, couple the near (throat) cut, pull n cars onto the lead.
      • SPOT n to a track — shove n cars in and leave them.
      • KICK n to a track — shove and cut away; the cars coast in on their own with no coupling. Only where a special instruction allows it (⚡), only onto a secured 2+ car cut, and only car types that can be kicked.

      Score = fewest engine MOVES — beat or match par. A move is one run of the engine in one direction, so every change of direction counts: a PULL = 2 (back in, pull out) and a SPOT = 2 (shove in, pull out) — except the spot that finishes the job is 1, because the engine just leaves the cars and is done (no pull-out). A KICK = 1 (one shove, the cars coast in by themselves). So pull, pull, spot = 5 moves, and kicking saves the pull-out. Joints (couplings) is a second, "also good" stat: lower is cleaner. To reach a track, line its switch reverse and the ones below it normal, or the engineer refuses (CROR 104).

      Securing & kicking. Rule 112 — any car left unattended gets a hand brake. A KICK (Rule 113.5) needs a secured cut of at least two cars as a backstop to catch the rolling car — and a tank, autorack or centrebeam is never kicked. After coupling, the engineer stretches the joint to confirm the knuckles are locked before moving (Rule 113.2).
